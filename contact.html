Before you call

Four things that change the quote.

  • Which rooms, and how many.Whole interior, one room, or a single wall. Ceilings counted separately — they are their own job.
  • What the light does.Big windows, grazing downlights or a long wall lit from one end all push the finish up a level.
  • What is going on afterwards.Flat paint forgives. Eggshell, satin, gloss and deep colours do not, and neither does wallpaper.
  • Whether it has been tried before.An old patch that was never feathered wide enough usually has to come back out. Say so — it is not held against you.

Questions

Straight answers.

What finish level do I need?

Level 4 for most rooms with flat or lightly textured paint. Level 5 if the wall takes raking light, grazing downlights, gloss or enamel paint, or a deep colour. The overview sets out all six levels so you can judge for yourself.

Will you match existing texture?

That is the harder half of any patch — matching what is already on the wall so the repair does not read as a repair. Talk it through on the call, ideally with a photo taken in side light.

How much dust is this going to make?

Sanding makes dust; the question is whether it is contained. Ask how it will be controlled and where it will be sanded — a vague answer there tells you what the rest of the job will look like.

Do you do whole homes or just single rooms?

Both. One reviewer's home "looks like a brand new house" afterwards, which is the larger end; a single patch is still a real job.
